How does the 80% margin rule work?

Updated 18 November 2026

You can't use more than 80% of your account's margin at once. This article shows how the margin on a trade is worked out, how traders go over the limit without noticing, and where to check your margin before you trade.

The rule

Margin usage = margin used by all your open trades ÷ your equity × 100

Equity includes the floating profit or loss on your open trades, so this figure changes while your trades are open.

Every open trade counts. If you have three trades open at the same time, their margin is added together and checked as one total.

How much margin a trade uses

Margin for one trade = lot size × contract size × price ÷ leverage

  • Forex: 1 lot is 100,000 units of the first currency in the pair.
  • Gold (XAUUSD): 1 lot is 100 ounces.
  • Indices and other symbols: contract sizes differ. Open the symbol's specification in your platform to see it.

Leverage depends on the phase (see What is the account leverage?):

Funded accounts start at half the evaluation leverage on forex, indices and commodities. The same trade uses twice as much margin once you're funded.

Example 1: one trade that is too big

$10,000 evaluation account. Gold is at $4,000 (a round number for the example). Leverage is 1:20.

  • 1 lot of gold uses 100 × $4,000 ÷ 20 = $20,000 of margin.
  • 0.30 lot uses $6,000. That's 60% of $10,000, so it's within the rule.
  • 0.45 lot uses $9,000. That's 90%, over the rule.

Example 2: several small trades together

Same account, same price. You open three gold trades of 0.15 lot each.

  • Each one uses $3,000, which is 30% on its own.
  • Together they use $9,000, which is 90%. Over the rule.

The rule looks at the total, not at each trade.

Example 3: the same lot size after you're funded

$10,000 funded account at 1:10. Gold at $4,000.

  • 1 lot of gold uses 100 × $4,000 ÷ 10 = $40,000 of margin.
  • 0.25 lot uses $10,000. That's 100%. Over the rule.

In the evaluation, the same 0.25 lot used $5,000, or 50%. A lot size that was fine in your evaluation can break the rule on your funded account.

Example 4: a losing trade pushes you over

$10,000 funded account at 1:50. You open 3.9 lots of USDJPY. The dollar is the first currency in the pair, so 1 lot is $100,000.

  • Margin: 3.9 × $100,000 ÷ 50 = $7,800. That's 78%, within the rule.
  • The trade goes $300 against you. Your equity drops to $9,700.
  • $7,800 ÷ $9,700 = 80.4%. You're over the rule without opening anything new.

If a floating loss alone takes you over, your trades were already close to the limit when you opened them.

Where to check your margin

Trading platforms don't show margin usage. They show Margin Level, which uses the same two numbers the other way round:

Margin Level = equity ÷ margin × 100

Margin usage (our rule)Margin Level (your platform)
50%200%
80%125%
100%100%

If your Margin Level is above 125%, you're under 80%.

  • MT5 desktop. Open the Toolbox (Ctrl+T) and go to the Trade tab. The line above your open trades shows Balance, Equity, Margin, Free Margin and Margin Level. Margin Level only appears while a trade is open.
  • MT5 mobile. Open the Trade tab. Balance, Equity, Margin, Free margin and Margin level are at the top.
  • cTrader. The TradeWatch panel at the bottom of the screen shows Balance, Equity, Margin, Free Margin and Margin Level.

What happens if you go over

Going over 80% doesn't close your trade or stop you opening new ones. Your trader dashboard shows flagged trades in the Flagged so far card, but the final amount is set when our Risk team reviews your payout.

On a funded account, our Risk team removes the profit made on flagged trades when it reviews your payout. Only profit is removed. A flagged trade that closed at a loss stays in your results. Depending on the case, our Risk team can instead move a funded account back to an evaluation phase.

On an evaluation account, nothing happens to your account while you trade. Moving from Phase 1 to Phase 2 is automatic. Our Risk team reviews flagged trades from all phases together when you pass your final phase, before your account goes live.

Risk looks at how much of your profit came from flagged trades. If it's a small amount, your pass stands. If it's a large part of your profit, Risk removes that profit and your pass isn't validated. You get a new account for your final phase (a new Phase 2 on a 2-Step, a new account on a 1-Step), and your trading days start again:

  • If you still have profit left after the deduction, it carries over to the new account.
  • If the deduction would take you below your starting balance, the new account starts at your starting balance.

Example: you pass Phase 2 of a $5,000 evaluation at $5,400. $350 of your profit came from flagged trades. Risk removes the $350 and your new Phase 2 starts at $5,050. If the flagged trades had made $500, removing it would leave you below $5,000, so your new Phase 2 would start at $5,000.
